Flight Test A

What to fly and what to check for the first test flight of this Edward build. Every test point says what the feature is, what to do, and what counts as a pass. You report the results in a form with the same numbers, setting each point to Passed, Not passed or Not tested.

What Edward is

Edward is the navigator in the right-hand seat of the DCS Mosquito F.B. Mk. VI. He calls out headings and times, keeps the fuel cocks right, arms the bombs and works the bomb bay on a bomb run, and can hold the aircraft on a course and altitude for you. You talk to him through a ring menu: press e in flight. Every wedge of that menu is explained in The Edward Menu.

Words used on this page

  • Hold (menu Hold My Aircraft): Edward flies the aircraft on a set course and altitude. Every test is flown on Hold; no hand flying is needed.
  • Steer call: Edward's on-screen line telling you the course to fly, for example “Two minutes to WP2. Steer 095°M, range 8.1 NM.”
  • Check Drift (menu Navigation ▸ Drift Recorder): Edward measures the real wind over about a minute. Before that he uses the weather forecast written into the mission.
  • IP / TGT: the Initial Point where the bomb run starts, and the Target. Edward finds them by the waypoint names “IP” and “TGT”.
  • Outer and main tanks: the Mosquito's wing tanks. Edward uses the outer tanks first and changes the main fuel cocks to MAIN (the inner tanks) when the outer ones are nearly empty. With drop tanks fitted, the transfer cock pumps drop-tank fuel into the outer tanks.
  • dcs.log: DCS's log file, Saved Games\<your DCS folder>\Logs\dcs.log. Some points ask you to search it for a line of text after the flight: open it in a text editor and use Find. DCS starts a new log each time it starts, so check it (and keep a copy for the report) before starting DCS again.

Before you start

  1. Install the test build (run Install.bat) and restart DCS.
  2. In flight, menu Status ▸ Logging: set it ON. The log checks below need it.
  3. Menu Settings ▸ Automations: set everything ON.

Part 1 — Mission A, slot A-Run: a bomb run before any wind measurement

This part checks the bomb run while Edward still only knows the forecast wind, so do not use Check Drift in this slot. Turn Hold ON and let the steer calls take you to the IP and on to the target.

1.1 IP and target found from the waypoint names

What Edward picks the IP and target himself from waypoints named “IP” and “TGT”. Nothing else in the mission marks the target.

Do Open the menu and look at the centre of the Weapons and Bombing page.

Pass It shows the two waypoints, for example WP4 >> WP5, with a planned time on target below.

1.2 A list with only one point still draws a full ring

What The ring menu can't draw fewer than three wedges as a full circle. A list holding one point used to leave an empty gap; now a Refresh wedge fills it.

Do Weapons and Bombing ▸ TGT ▸ TGT Waypoint, then the same under IP.

Pass Each shows the one waypoint, Refresh and Go Back as a full ring with no empty sector.

1.3 Only the stations carrying bombs are armed

What At the IP Edward arms the bombs. He should select only the stations that carry bombs (the two in the bomb bay), not the wing stations, which carry drop tanks.

Do Fly through the IP; Edward says “Bombs armed”. Look at the bomb station switches, or Weapons and Bombing ▸ Arm Weapons ▸ Select Bombs.

Pass The two bomb-bay stations are selected; the two wing stations are not.

1.4 IP course uses the forecast wind (log check)

What At the IP Edward works out the course to the target, corrected for wind. With no wind measured yet he uses the mission's forecast wind, and says so.

Do After the flight, search dcs.log for IP course to target.

Pass The line is there and ends with (met forecast wind).

1.5 No steering in the last minute before the target

What Real Mosquito crews flew the last part of a bomb run by eye, and inside the last minute it is too late for course or speed corrections. So from one minute out Edward stops steering. He only counts down to the target and calls the bomb bay; later builds add altitude calls there too.

Do Navigation must be on the TGT waypoint. It moves on along the route by itself; if not, pick TGT in Navigation ▸ By Waypoints. Watch the calls as the target approaches.

Pass Up to about two minutes out the calls include “Steer …”. From one minute out you see only “One minute to TGT.”, “30 seconds.” and “10 seconds to TGT.”. There's no “Off track” call and no “Turn … for WP6” heading in that last minute.

1.6 Bomb bay opens about 4 NM before the target

What Edward opens the bomb bay doors about one minute (4 NM) before the target.

Pass “Opening the bomb bay.” about 4 NM out (the range is shown on the steer calls and the Navigation page).

1.7 Bombs away ends the run, navigation carries on

What When the bombs are gone Edward closes the bay and ends the run.

Do Release the bombs over the target with the bomb release key.

Pass “Bombs away.” and “Closing the bomb bay.” come, and afterwards the normal steer calls to the next waypoint (HOME) resume.

Part 2 — Mission A, slot A-Long: the long flight

Three 40 NM legs at 6,000 ft, then a climb, then the same bomb run as Part 1. Several points need a watch or the in-game clock: note the time of your first Check Drift.

2.1 Dead Reckoning menu right after loading

What Using the menu in the first seconds after loading, before Edward's mission side is ready, must not cause an error.

Do Within 2–3 seconds of the mission starting, open the menu and click Navigation ▸ DR Landmarks.

Pass No Lua error box. A short “not ready” message, or nothing at all, is fine. Note what you saw.

2.2 Forecast wind is marked as such

What Before any Check Drift, Edward's wind correction comes from the forecast, and he should say so.

Do Turn Hold ON towards WP1 and read the steer calls, then open the Navigation page.

Pass The steer calls end with “(met forecast wind)”, and the centre of the Navigation page shows “Met forecast”.

2.3 Fuel economy wedge text

Do Open the Fuel page.

Pass The wedge reads “AMPG at this setting?” on one line, not broken over two.

2.4 One map point still draws a full ring

What Same as 1.2, for map points.

Do Place one mark on the F10 map and name it NAV. Then open Navigation ▸ By Map Points and Fuel ▸ Fuel to Point ▸ To Map Point.

Pass Both show NAV and Refresh (and Go Back) as a full ring with no empty sector.

2.5 Radio tuning across wavebands, no false warning

What Edward tunes the R1155 receiver and switches its waveband when needed. Some wavebands overlap, and there he used to log a false “band switch did not take” warning.

Do Radio ▸ Manually Tune, one after the other: 12.0, 4.0, 7.5, 0.2, 1.0 MHz. Wait for each one to finish.

Pass Each one tunes, and after the flight dcs.log contains neither did not take nor FAILED for these tunes.

2.6 After a wind measurement, the forecast note goes

Do On leg 1, Navigation ▸ Drift Recorder ▸ Check Drift, and fly straight for about a minute until the result shows. Write down the time.

Pass The following steer calls no longer end with “(met forecast wind)”, and the Navigation page no longer shows “Met forecast”.

2.7 Landmark position fixes (their first test)

What Edward knows towns, river crossings and railway features along your route (“DR landmarks”). As you pass one he warns you, then tells you where you are against your planned track: early or late, and left or right of track.

Do Fly legs 1 and 2 on Hold and watch the calls.

Pass You get a heads-up before a landmark and a fix call at it. In the notes, say whether the distances and the early/late and left/right figures seemed believable.

2.8 Ten minutes of steady Hold

What A long straight leg on Hold, to see how steadily it holds height and wings level.

Do On leg 2, leave the aircraft on Hold, hands off, for about 10 minutes.

Pass No height dips or slow roll-offs you would notice from the cockpit. In the notes, describe any you saw (how big, roughly when); that is the main thing this point is after.

2.9 An old wind measurement is flagged

What A wind measured more than 30 minutes ago may no longer be right, and Edward should say so.

Do Fly on without another Check Drift until 30 minutes after the time you wrote down at 2.6.

Pass The steer calls then end with “(drift estimate stale - check drift again)”.

2.10 A big height change makes the wind measurement stale

What The wind changes with height, so after a big climb a wind measured lower down no longer applies.

Do Check Drift once more, then on leg 3 Hold My Aircraft ▸ Set Altitude 20000 and let it climb.

Pass During or after the climb the steer calls end with “(drift estimate stale - check drift again)”.

2.11 Fuel cocks change with height

What Above 10,000 ft Edward pressurises the tanks (venting cock ON) and feeds from the main tanks. Back below 7,500 ft the venting cock goes OFF again.

Do Watch the venting cock and the main fuel cocks (or the Fuel page) during the climb and the later descent.

Pass Above 10,000 ft venting ON and main cocks on MAIN; below 7,500 ft venting OFF.

2.12 Drop-tank transfer starts together with the venting cock

What When the outer tanks are down to 25 gal, Edward turns on the transfer cock to refill them from the drop tanks, and the venting cock with it, 1–2 seconds apart.

Do This happens by itself whenever the outer tanks reach 25 gal. It may not happen on this flight; then leave it at Not tested. Part 3 covers it too.

Pass The transfer and venting cocks go ON one after the other, 1–2 s apart.

2.13 Second bomb run uses the measured wind (log check)

Do Descend to 2,000 ft before the IP and fly the bomb run as in Part 1.

Pass In dcs.log, this run's IP course to target line does not end with “(met forecast wind)”, because a measured wind now exists. Points 1.3–1.7 behave as before.

2.14 Restarting the mission resets Edward (log check)

Do At the very end, press Shift-R to restart the mission.

Pass dcs.log contains a line with mission reload ending in fuel figures and pending cock clicks reset.

Part 3 — Mission FuelLow: outer tanks running low

Turn Hold ON and touch nothing; Edward handles the fuel cocks. Watch the main fuel cocks and the outer tank gauges (or the Fuel page).

3.1 No drop tanks: one clean change to MAIN at 8 gal

What The Mosquito Pilot's Notes say to use the outer tanks first and change to the main tanks when they are nearly empty. Edward does that at 8 gal.

Do Slot B-NoTanks. Fly until the outer tanks are below 8 gal.

Pass The main fuel cocks move from OUTER to MAIN once, at about 8 gal, and never flip back; both engines keep running. dcs.log has outer tanks low.

3.2 With drop tanks: stay on MAIN and start the transfer

What With drop tanks fitted and the outer tanks low, Edward feeds from the main tanks and refills the outer tanks from the drop tanks.

Do Slot B-Tanks, then watch the cocks.

Pass The main fuel cocks go to MAIN and stay there, and the transfer cock and venting cock go ON, 1–2 s apart.

3.3 Refilled: back to the outer tanks

Do Keep flying until the outer tanks have refilled above 25 gal (a few minutes).

Pass The main fuel cocks go back to OUTER, and dcs.log has low-outer latch released.

Part 4 — Mission DRSurvey: saving landmark points

This test saves Edward's Normandy landmark file, so back it up first: copy Saved Games\<your DCS folder>\Scripts\Edward\DRPoints\Normandy.lua somewhere safe.

4.2 Saving shows a result on the menu

Do Open Navigation ▸ DR Landmarks. Delete one point (or use Re-survey Theatre), then Save.

Pass The centre of the page shows SAVED n + n YOURS (two numbers).

4.3 Put the backup back

Copy your backed-up Normandy.lua back into DRPoints, so normal missions use the original points again.

There is no 4.1 in this test: that number is a check done by the developer.
