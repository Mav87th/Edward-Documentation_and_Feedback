Dead Reckoning — Survey Notes

These notes describe the Dead Reckoning survey facility of EDWARD, a DCS World simulator modification for the De Havilland Mosquito F.B. Mk. VI. They are complementary to the Edward Menu Notes and assume a thorough knowledge of its contents. They are prepared for the guidance of the player only, and create no obligation beyond the simulator itself.

✍ Note.— This publication describes Phase 1 of the Dead Reckoning facility only: the theatre survey and its curation upon the F10 map (Parts II–IV below). The automatic selection of Dead Reckoning points during a live sortie has not yet been brought into service; see Operating Data at the end of this publication.

Notes to users

1. Complementary publication

These notes are complementary to the Edward Menu Notes and assume a thorough knowledge of its contents, and that EDWARD has already been installed and found to be working correctly.

2. Nature of this publication

This publication describes EDWARD, a DCS World simulator modification for the Mosquito F.B. Mk. VI, and not the aeroplane itself; nothing in it creates any obligation beyond the simulator.

3. Conventions

Throughout this publication the following conventions apply:— words in capital letters indicate the actual wording shown upon a menu wedge, or, where marked DR - or FP -, upon an F10 map mark; these prefixes are not to be altered.

4. Binding force

The limitations quoted in Part III are mandatory. The contents of the other parts are mainly advisory, but instructions containing the words “is to be” and “are to be” are also mandatory.

5. Phase 1 only

This publication describes Phase 1 of the Dead Reckoning facility only. The automatic selection of Dead Reckoning points during a live sortie (Phase 2) has not yet been brought into service; see Operating Data.

6. The player

Throughout, “the player” denotes the person at the controls.

Part I — Descriptive

7. Dead Reckoning points

Dead Reckoning points (D.R. points) are landmarks — churches, bridges, railway stations and depots, rail junctions and yards, water crossings, bunkers and radar stations, stadiums, and airfields — collected for a theatre so that EDWARD may take a fix upon them in flight. Each theatre's points are held in a file of its own, one file to a theatre, at Scripts\Edward\DRPoints\<theatre>.lua.

8. Classification

Each point is classified Low, Medium, or High, according to how close a landmark must be approached before it can be trusted as a fix. Medium is given automatically unless a point is placed by hand with the band stated; a hand-placed point left unmarked for band is also Medium. See Operating Data for how this classification is intended to be used once brought into service.

9. Points placed by hand

A landmark may also be added directly by the player: an ordinary F10 map mark whose text begins FP -, e.g. FP - Church. Such a point is accepted without question; EDWARD does not verify what is on the ground there. If the player states a band — FP - Church - Low — the point is intended to be treated exactly as an automatic point of that band once Operating Data is brought into service; if no band is stated, Medium is given as for any other point. In a mission, such a point loses its FP - prefix and any band, and is shown simply as, e.g., DR - Church, exactly as an automatically found point.

10. The status/action wedge

The status/action wedge under Navigation › By D.R. Landmarks is marked CREATE DR POINTS, SURVEYING NN%, VIEW ON MAP (N), SAVE (N UNSAVED), or SAVE DR POINTS, according to the state of the theatre's survey. Words in capital letters indicate the actual wording shown upon the wedge. See Part II for the sequence in which each marking is reached.

Part II — Handling

11. Preparing the mission

A theatre is surveyed once only, not on every sortie. Before the survey is begun, a mission should be prepared consisting of the Mosquito alone, cold at any airfield on the theatre concerned, with no route and no other unit placed.

Note.— No safeguard yet prevents the survey facility being invoked in an ordinary mission; the player alone is responsible for choosing a suitable one.

12. Opening the survey

(i) Fly is to be clicked to enter the cockpit, as for any mission; there is no need to take off.

(ii) The Edward Menu (not DCS's own radio menu) is to be opened and Navigation › By D.R. Landmarks selected.

(iii) If the theatre has not before been surveyed, the wedge is marked CREATE DR POINTS. It should be clicked once only: EDWARD then tiles the whole theatre with an anchor approximately every 5 n.m. and searches about each of these automatically, and the wedge marking changes to SURVEYING NN% while this proceeds.

(iv) If the theatre has already been surveyed, the wedge is marked VIEW ON MAP (N), N being the number of points already held; clicking it places them upon the F10 map without altering the file. There is no separate facility for a further automatic search; further coverage is to be had only by the player's own FP - points (para. 9).

13. During the survey

(i) Nothing further need be done; the search proceeds of itself while EDWARD works through the theatre, and results appear upon the F10 map as they are found.

(ii) A large theatre may take some while, EDWARD working through it methodically rather than by following anything the player does.

(iii) Curation (para. 14) may be begun upon points already found without waiting for the whole theatre to be finished.

14. Curating the map

Once points appear, the F10 map is the working copy, and is to be edited directly before saving:

(i) A point wrongly placed is to be deleted like any other F10 mark.

(ii) A landmark missed by the automatic search may be added by hand, as an FP - mark (para. 9).

(iii) A badly placed automatic point is corrected by combining (i) and (ii): the bad mark deleted, a fresh FP - mark added in its place.

(iv) There is no facility to discard curation directly; leaving the mission without saving has the same effect (Part IV).

15. Saving

The wedge, once any point has been added or deleted, is marked SAVE (N UNSAVED) — N being the number of changes pending, not the total held — or SAVE DR POINTS where the changes pending net to none. Clicking it writes the F10 map's present state to the theatre's file. This is to be understood as a replacement of the whole file, not a merger of it with what is on the map (Part III). A single backup of the previous file is kept, as <theatre>.lua.bak; it should not be relied upon for more than the one save immediately before it (Part III). Saving at intervals during a long survey, rather than only at the end, is recommended.

16. Ending the survey

Once saved, nothing further need be done; the mission may be left. The same sequence, beginning again at VIEW ON MAP, may be returned to at any time to curate the theatre further.

Part III — Limitations

The following limitations are to be observed during all conditions of use of the Dead Reckoning survey facility:—

17. Saving replaces, it does not merge

Saving is to be understood as replacing the whole of a theatre's Dead Reckoning Points file with exactly what stands upon the F10 map at the moment SAVE is clicked; there is no facility for merging old and new. The player is to satisfy himself that the map is correct — points wrongly placed removed, points missed added — before saving, not after.

18. One backup only

A single generation of backup only is kept (<theatre>.lua.bak); it is overwritten at every subsequent save and does not accumulate. It is not to be relied upon to recover more than the one save immediately before it.

19. Theatre-wide, not route-specific

A survey covers the whole of the theatre in a single pass, not merely the neighbourhood of any one route. The Dead Reckoning Points file is common to every mission flown afterward upon that theatre, and is not to be confused with data belonging to any one mission.

20. Theatres provided for

Theatre boundaries are known in advance for the following theatres only: Afghanistan, Caucasus, the Falklands, Germany (Cold War), Iraq, Kola, the Mariana Islands, the Marianas (WWII), Nevada, Normandy, the Persian Gulf, Sinai, Syria, and The Channel. Of these, only Normandy has been surveyed and confirmed in flight.

Part IV — Emergencies

21. Interruption of the search

Should the player click the status/action wedge while SURVEYING NN% is shown, no further action is taken beyond a report of the percentage completed; the search is not to be started twice, and cannot be hastened by repeated clicking.

22. Leaving before saving

Should the mission be left, or DCS closed, before SAVE has been clicked, the theatre's file on disk remains exactly as it stood before the survey was begun; no curation done in that session is lost from the file, because none of it was ever written to it. Nor can it be recovered for a later session: a mission left unsaved must be curated afresh from what the map then shows.

23. Recovery from a wrong deletion

A point deleted in error is not restored automatically. It is to be replaced by hand, as a fresh FP - mark placed in the same position (para. 9); it will then be treated as any other player-placed point once saved.

24. Recovery from a bad save

Should a save prove to have been made in error, the single backup described in para. 18 is the only recourse, and only for the save immediately preceding it. Beyond that, there is no facility for recovery, and a fresh survey (para. 12) is the only remedy.

Operating data — not yet in service

25. General

The following describes the intended use of Dead Reckoning points once brought into service; none of it is presently in effect. It is placed here, and not under Limitations, because it does not yet bind: nothing below happens automatically in a mission today.

26. Automatic selection (intended)

It is intended that, upon a mission being loaded on a surveyed theatre, EDWARD will himself examine the theatre's Dead Reckoning Points file for those points lying near the route flown, and mark the useful ones upon the F10 map without the player's intervention. A second wedge is intended for this purpose, additional to that described in the Check List, together with a further picker, By D.R. Points, alongside By Waypoints and By Map Points.

27. Selection by height (intended)

It is intended that a point be offered according to the height at which that leg is to be flown:—

(i) Below 3,000 ft. A.G.L., only Low points close to the track are to be offered.

(ii) Between 3,000 ft. and 12,000 ft. A.G.L., both Low and High points are to be offered, save that a small feature lying directly beneath the track is set aside in favour of one to a side, a large and unmistakable feature excepted.

(iii) Above 12,000 ft. A.G.L., only High points are to be offered.

(iv) A point placed by hand without a stated band (para. 9) is to be offered regardless of height; one given a band is to be treated exactly as an automatic point of that band.

Note.— This scheme was drafted before the Medium classification (para. 8) was adopted for the survey itself, and how the two are to be reconciled is not yet settled.
