The Edward Menu

These notes describe the Edward Menu, the overlay control panel of EDWARD, a DCS World simulator modification for the De Havilland Mosquito F.B. Mk. VI. They are complementary to the Installation Notes for fitting EDWARD in the first place, and stand on their own for the menu itself; By DR Landmarks is described in fuller detail by the separate Dead Reckoning Survey Notes, which are themselves complementary to this publication. They are prepared for the guidance of the player only, and create no obligation beyond the simulator itself. A demonstration of the menu, copied directly from its own source, is given at the foot of these notes.

Notes to users

1. Complementary publications

This publication is complementary to the Installation Notes, and assumes EDWARD has already been installed and found to be working correctly. The Dead Reckoning Survey Notes are complementary to this publication, and describe By DR Landmarks in fuller detail than is given here.

2. Nature of this publication

This publication describes EDWARD, a DCS World simulator modification for the Mosquito F.B. Mk. VI, and not the aeroplane itself; nothing in it creates any obligation beyond the simulator. Throughout, words in capital letters indicate the actual wording shown upon a menu wedge, and “the player” denotes the person at the controls.

3. Binding force

The limitations stated below are mandatory. The remainder is mainly advisory, but instructions containing the words “is to be” and “are to be” are also mandatory.

The Edward Menu

4. The Edward Menu

The menu is a radial dial superimposed upon the cockpit, opened and closed with the “e” key while flying; it is available only during flight in the Mosquito F.B. Mk. VI, and not in the Mission Editor or the main menu. It is fitted with seven root wedges, described below at 4.1–4.7. Stepping back — by right-click, or by a wedge marked Go Back where one is fitted — rebuilds the parent ring afresh from current data, exactly as drilling into it does. Where a ring calls for a number — Manually Tune under Radio, or Set Track and Set Altitude under Hold My Aircraft — digits are to be clicked to build it, and the centre hub clicked to confirm; confirming always closes the menu outright, never re-showing any ring.

Wedge wording is stamped in placard capitals, a full stop being drawn as a middle dot and a hyphen held unbroken, so that no marking is broken across a line. The centre hub reads Edward; where a ring has a figure of its own to report — the survey under By DR Landmarks, or the A.M.P.G. measurement under Fuel — it is given in the hub rather than upon a wedge. Lists longer than a ring will carry are paged: route waypoints five to a ring, from WP0 to WP14, and radio preset letters eight to a ring, the letters I and O being passed over so that they are not mistaken for the figures 1 and 0. A digit ring is composed of 0–9 and DEL, a decimal point being added where a frequency or a run-in figure calls for one.

Note.— In the markings given at 4.1–4.7 below, wording set in capitals is the wedge's own; wording set thus stands for a figure or a name filled in by EDWARD as the wedge is drawn.

4.1 Status

Two groups are given, both read-only: Weather (Wind, Temp, QNH) and Aircraft (Ammo, Damage). Each reading is given immediately upon the wedge being clicked, and the ring remains open afterward, so that several readings may be taken in succession.

Markings
  • Weather — Wind, Temp, QNH
  • Aircraft — Ammo, Damage

4.2 Settings

Distance, Headings, Speed, and Altitude are each a simple choice among the units offered — Nautical Miles or Kilometers; Magnetic or True; Knots, M.P.H., or K.P.H.; Feet or Metres. Automations lists every action EDWARD is able to take of his own accord: a master switch for all of them, and one switch each for Fuel Management, Arming Management, Pitot Heat, Radiator Management, Bombbay Automation, and Nav Updates (see Check List). Bombbay Automation governs whether EDWARD works the bomb doors himself during a run, separate from Arming Management, which gates the run controller as a whole. Nav Updates governs whether EDWARD calls out navigation information at all; it is already turned on the moment a waypoint or map point is selected under Navigation, so this switch chiefly serves to turn it off again. Choosing a unit, or any automation switch, is to be understood as resetting the menu to its root and leaving it open, Bombbay Automation excepted, which re-shows the Automations ring itself instead.

Markings
  • Distance — unit — Nautical Miles, Kilometers
  • Headings — mode — Magnetic, True
  • Speed — unit — Knots, MPH, KPH
  • Altitude — unit — Feet, Meters
  • Automations, each wedge a sentence giving the state it stands in:
    • All Automatics are ON / are OFF
    • Fuel Management is ON / is OFF
    • Arming Management is ON / is OFF
    • Pitot Heat is ON / is OFF
    • Radiator Management is ON / is OFF
    • Bombbay Automation is ON / is OFF
    • Nav Updates are ON / are OFF

4.3 Fuel

Time Left gives the remaining flight time and gallons for All, Outer, or Mains, the overall burn rate being used regardless of which tank the engines are actually drawing from. A.M.P.G. measures true fuel efficiency: it is clicked, a timed reading of about fifteen seconds is taken, and it is clicked again for the result. Both rings remain open after a reading, as at 4.1 above. Fuel to Point gives the gallons and time required to reach the Waypoint, Homebase, or Map Point selected, calculated the moment the ring is opened and re-checked upon a further click. Homebase is EDWARD's own determination of the flight's starting airfield, found by a fourfold test — the nearest field to a cold or hot start; failing that, the first or last waypoint if authored as a ground point; failing that, the nearest friendly field there is.

Note.— The distances governing this test have not been confirmed in flight; the airfield found is usually, but not certainly, correct.

Markings
  • Time Left — All, Outer, Mains, each giving its reading upon the wedge once clicked
  • Calculate AMPG, which reads Recalculate AMPG once a measurement has been taken
  • Fuel to Point —
    • To Waypoint — WP0–WP14, five to a ring, with Refresh
    • To Homebase, reading Homebase not detected where the field is yet to be determined
    • To Map Point — one wedge for each marker held: IP, TGT, NAV, HOME, NEAREST

4.4 Radio

The T-1154 transmitter and the R-1155 receiver are each governed independently, each having a power switch, a Manually Tune digit ring (para. 4), and a Predefined Frequencies list covering all twenty-four channels laid down in the Mission Editor for that radio, each shown with its preset letter and frequency. The R-1155 may be tuned to any frequency typed; the T-1154 is to be set only to one of its fixed presets, and a frequency typed upon it is snapped to the nearest of these.

Manual Radio Tuning is fitted as a fourth wedge and hands the sets over to the player entirely: the menu closes, the player is moved to the navigator's seat, and the camera is brought upon the radio panel, the player working the sets by hand and returning to the pilot's seat in their own way.

Markings
  • T-1154 Transmitter is ON / is OFF
  • Tune T-1154 Transmitter — Manually Tune, Predefined Frequencies
  • Tune R-1155 Receiver — the same pair of wedges
  • Predefined Frequencies is laid out in three ranges, each of eight channels: Range 1 (10.0 - 5.5 Mc), Range 2 (5.5 - 3.0 Mc), Range 3 (500 - 200 Kc); each channel carries its preset letter and frequency, as A — freq
  • Manual Radio Tuning

4.5 Navigation

By Waypoints and By Map Points list the route and the map markers presently held, each refreshed automatically upon being opened. Selecting one begins navigation to it and turns on navigation callouts if they were not already on, then resets the menu to its root and leaves it open. While the ring itself is open, its centre hub shows the target's tag and steering heading, and, for a route waypoint, the speed required; a map point carries no planned leg time, so its speed is left out.

Drift Recorder is a single wedge, Check Drift. Clicking it closes the menu outright, and the check proceeds in the background:—

(a) a mantle of about ten seconds is followed by a measuring window of about forty seconds, with a “Steady” callout given at intervals;

(b) Drift, Groundspeed, and Estimated Wind are then reported in turn, a few seconds apart, each by toast;

(c) should any other action be taken elsewhere in the menu while the check is running, it is interrupted early and no reading is given for that attempt; drilling into a submenu or stepping back does not interrupt it.

The last completed reading is shown in the centre hub upon re-entering the ring, though it is not to be understood as live while the ring sits open.

By D.R. Landmarks is fitted here as a fourth wedge and is described fully in the Dead Reckoning Survey Notes. Its wedge and hub both follow the survey as it goes forward, from the order to create the points, through the survey itself, to the count of points saved and shown upon the map.

Markings
  • By Waypoints — WP0–WP14, five to a ring, with Refresh
  • By Map Points — IP, TGT, NAV, HOME, NEAREST
  • Drift-Recorder — Check Drift, which reads Checking Drift while one is in hand, and Cancel Check alongside it
  • By DR Landmarks — Create DR Points → Surveying nn% → Save DR Points or Save (n unsaved) → View on Map (n), with Clear Marks

4.6 Hold My Aircraft

Hold turns EDWARD's holding of a fixed heading and altitude on or off. Set Track and Set Altitude are digit rings, worked as at 4.4 above. Set Track accepts 0° to 359° only; Set Altitude accepts 0 to 60,000 (feet or metres, according to the unit chosen); a value outside these is not accepted.

Markings
  • Hold is ON / is OFF
  • Set Track (ref) — digit ring, the heading reference in force being carried upon the wedge
  • Set Altitude (unit) — digit ring, the altitude unit in force being carried upon the wedge

4.7 Weapons and Bombing

TGT and IP are each chosen from a route Waypoint or a Map Point. Where the mission carries a waypoint named TGT or IP, EDWARD chooses it of his own accord as soon as the mission's route is known to him — once only in a mission, and only where none has already been chosen by hand, which always overrides it. He announces the pair upon the screen as he does so, together with the planned time on target where the Mission Editor supplies one, so that no visit to this page is needed to learn of it; a Map Point must always be placed and chosen by hand. TGT's list of waypoints includes both those matched by name and any carrying a DCS Bombing task, the latter marked with a trailing asterisk; IP is matched by name only, a Bombing task marking the point of attack, not of run-in. In choosing of his own accord he takes the route in order of authority: a waypoint named exactly TGT or IP; failing that, one whose name merely begins with those letters; failing that, and for TGT alone, the first waypoint carrying a Bombing task. The point at which the aircraft begins its flight is not offered upon this page at all, being neither a target nor a point of run-in.

Arm Weapons governs the physical setup: Select Bombs toggles each of the four bomb stations, and Select Fuzes toggles Nose and Tail fuzing, both reading the actual cockpit switches, so that a switch worked by hand in the cockpit is shown here too. A final wedge shows the state of the run and doubles as the means of starting or stopping it. Upon a run being stopped, the mission's own TGT and IP are restored a few seconds later rather than the page being left empty; the run itself does not begin afresh until the aircraft has left the IP and come upon it again. Choosing a TGT or IP lands upon the Weapons and Bombing root; toggling a station or fuze lands upon Select Bombs or Select Fuzes itself, so that the freshly altered marking is seen at once.

Run In Altitude and Run In Speed are fitted under the map-point branches, a map point carrying no authored run-in figures of its own; each is a digit ring, and once a figure is given the wedge carries it thereafter. Where the route or the map holds nothing matching the name sought, the ring offers the whole of it instead, as All Waypoints or All Map Points.

Note.— Jettison is fitted but does not yet operate anything, and is not to be relied upon; clicking it explains only that those controls have not been live-tested.

Markings
  • TGT — selection — TGT Waypoint, TGT MapPoint; failing a match, All Waypoints or All Map Points
  • IP — selection — IP Waypoint, IP MapPoint, upon the same plan
  • Under either map-point branch, Run In Altitude and Run In Speed, reading Run In Alt nnnn and Run In Spd nnn once given
  • Arm Weapons —
    • Bombs — Select Bombs (St.1 Select / St.1 Selected, and so to St.4) and Select Fuzes (Nose is ON / is OFF, Tail is ON / is OFF)
    • Jettison
  • The state of the run, upon the root wedge: No Target Set → Stop the Run → Stop the Run (armed) → Stop the Run (RUN ON) → Securing...
